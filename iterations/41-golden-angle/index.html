THE PLACEMENT LAW

Every galaxy page stands on one frozen substrate. A permanent key is a point, and its place is a pure function of the key alone. This page takes the law apart with the page's own import of lib.mjs, and measures each claim while you watch.

1 · Why √key: equal area holds equal countWAIT

The keys inside radius r are the keys with SPACING·√key ≤ r, so their number grows as r². Cut the disc into rings of equal area and each ring should hold the same count: density on screen is density in the numbering. Below, every point is placed with placeAll() and assigned to a ring by its measured radius hypot(x, y), then counted.

2 · Why the golden anglecomputed

The fraction of a turn that separates consecutive keys decides whether keys line up. If the fraction is a rational p/q, key k and key k+q point the same way and the disc becomes q straight spokes. If it is irrational, no two keys ever share a ray, but a fraction close to a rational p/q still builds near-spokes once N is large. How close an irrational comes to rationals is read from its continued fraction: large terms mean a very good rational approximation. The golden fraction's terms are all ones after the first, the smallest possible, so it stays away from every rational as far as any number can.

How arms are measured: for every key in the outer half (key > N/2) its nearest neighbour is found through a grid bucket (cells of side SPACING, searched ring by ring, never all pairs). The most common key difference d between a key and its nearest neighbour is the number of arms: keys k, k+d, k+2d… lie on one arm. Each step along an arm turns it by the signed fractional part of d·(angle/2π) of a turn; over the disc an arm takes about N/d steps. The arms are called straight spokes when that total turn is less than half the gap between neighbouring arms (180°/d), and spiral arms otherwise.

3 · Why a key's place never changescomputed

Place is a function of the key and nothing else, so adding keys cannot move an existing one. Take a snapshot, move N (up to the full numbering), and compare. "Before" coordinates are read out of placeAll() over keys 1…Nbefore; "after" out of placeAll() over 1…Nafter, the Float32 values the GPU receives. A key beyond Nbefore already had its place: place() is defined for every positive integer.

Questions
1. How does this help draw a system or a single-line diagram?
It draws no busbar, feeder, transformer, cable route, protection or earthing: element none. It is the addressing substrate every drawing on the wafer uses. place(key) in lib.mjs turns the permanent key (the field read from all-lines.bin) into x, y. Because that address never moves, a code card, a layer (whose geometry holds keys, never coordinates) and a deep link ?line= land on the same point and agree about which line they mean.
